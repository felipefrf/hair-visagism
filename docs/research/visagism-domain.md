## 1. Visagism as a Discipline

### 1.1 Origins and definition

**Visagism** (Portuguese: *visagismo*, from French *visage*, "face") is the practice of designing a personal image — hair, makeup, eyewear, even dental restorations — so that the visual language of the face communicates the person's identity, personality, and desired social message. The term traces to French hairdresser **Fernand Aubry** (mid-20th century), who coined *visagisme* for the artistic treatment of the face. The modern, systematized discipline, however, was developed by **Philip Hallawell**, a British-Brazilian artist and educator, who began his work in London in 1971 and formalized the method in Brazil (his book *Visagismo: Harmonia e Estética*, 2003, is the field's foundational text). Hallawell insists that his *visagismo* is a distinct, broader discipline from Aubry's *visagisme*: not just beautification, but the deliberate creation of a personal image using the grammar of visual language ([visagismo.com.br — History of Visagism](https://visagismo.com.br/index.php/en/visagism-en-uk/about-visagism/history-of-visagismo); [Visagismo is not Visagisme](https://visagismo.com.br/index.php/en/visagism-en-uk/about-visagism/visagismo-is-not-visagisme); [Visual language and personal image](https://visagismo.com.br/index.php/en/visual-language-and-the-construction-of-a-personal-image?layout=blog)).

Hallawell's method fuses:

- **Hippocrates' / Galen's four-temperament theory** (c. 460 BC) with **Jungian psychology** (archetypes, psychological types);
- **Gestalt / visual-arts theory of composition** — the idea that lines, shapes, angles, proportions, and colors carry universal symbolic meanings that the brain decodes pre-verbally;
- **Facial morphology analysis** — reading the dominant lines and shapes of a person's face as expressions of their temperament.

The method has been influential well beyond hairdressing: it is cited in the dental literature as the basis of "smile visagism" — designing tooth shapes to express personality (Paolucci et al., *Visagism: The Art of Dental Composition*, QDT 2012 — see [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/289751541/Paolucci-Visagism-QDT); [WJARR 2023 review, "Visagism in Dentistry"](https://wjarr.com/sites/default/files/WJARR-2023-0951.pdf); [SlideShare summary](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/visagism-the-art-of-dental-composition/58233648)). This dental literature is actually the best English-language documentation of Hallawell's line-symbolism system.

### 1.2 Core principle: the semantics of lines, shapes, and angles

Visagism's central claim: **every visual element carries an emotional/personality connotation**, and a face (or haircut) is "read" as a composition of such elements. The canonical mappings, consistent across Hallawell's writing, the dental-visagism papers, and general design psychology ([The Language of Lines](https://www.lazarova.art/post/the-language-of-lines); [Psychology of Line, Shape, Colors](https://angelino.us/blogs/suits/psychology-of-line-shape-colors-textures-size-and-fit-in-design); [CHI education — Haircutting Using Lines and Angles](https://education.chi.com/online-courses/chapter-9-haircutting/lessons/haircutting-using-lines-and-angles/)):

| Visual element | Perceived meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vertical straight lines** | Strength, power, authority, ambition, masculinity |
| **Horizontal straight lines** | Stability, calm, balance, passivity, trustworthiness |
| **Diagonal / oblique lines** | Dynamism, movement, energy, excitement, instability, youth |
| **Curved lines** | Softness, warmth, sensuality, gentleness, approachability, femininity |
| **Sharp angles / points** | Aggressiveness, boldness, decisiveness, drama; in excess, hostility |
| **Square / rectangular shapes** | Solidity, rationality, conservatism, reliability |
| **Round / oval shapes** | Friendliness, harmony, empathy, diplomacy |
| **Triangular shapes (point up)** | Ambition, dynamism, creativity |
| **Symmetry & proportion** | Beauty, order, competence |
| **Asymmetry** | Creativity, unconventionality, artistic identity |

The same grammar applies to hair: a **blunt, straight-line cut** (e.g., a geometric bob with straight fringe) reads as strong/decisive; a **layered, wavy, curved cut** reads as warm/romantic; an **asymmetric or diagonal cut** reads as dynamic/creative; **texture and volume** read as informality and spontaneity, while **sleek, controlled hair** reads as discipline and professionalism.

### 1.3 The four temperaments in visagism

Hallawell classifies clients by dominant temperament (usually a primary + secondary mix), diagnosed through facial morphology, gesture, speech, and a questionnaire, then designs the image either to **express** that temperament or to strategically **compensate** toward the image the client needs (e.g., softening an overly severe look). Sources: [Hallawell's Method of Temperament Analysis](https://visagismo.com.br/index.php/en/visagism-en-uk/about-visagism/method-of-temperament-analysis); [Choleric Type example](https://visagismo.com.br/index.php/en/technique-and-style?view=article&id=198:choleric-type&catid=102); [All Things Hair BR — temperaments](https://www.allthingshair.com/pt-br/penteados-cortes/todos-os-tipos-de-rosto/quatro-tipos-de-temperamento-no-visagismo-descubra-o-seu/); [Imagética — temperamentos do visagismo](https://imagetica.art/quais-sao-os-temperamentos-do-visagismo/); [Bernardo Lins — temperamentos no visagismo](https://www.bernardolins.com.br/temperamentos-no-visagismo/); [Isabella Mirindiba](https://www.isabellamirindiba.com/post/os-temperamentos-do-visagismo); [Sephora BR blog](https://blog.sephora.com.br/o-que-e-temperamento-de-beleza/); [Nana Machado](http://nanamachado.com.br/site/o-que-sao-os-temperamentos-do-visagismo/).

| | **Sanguine ("dynamic")** | **Choleric ("strong")** | **Melancholic ("sensitive")** | **Phlegmatic ("calm")** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Element / color** | Air; **yellow**, warm bright colors | Fire; **red**, intense saturated colors | Earth; **blue/violet**, soft cool/pastel tones | Water; **green**, neutral, muted tones |
| **Personality read** | Extroverted, communicative, fun, spontaneous, restless, loves novelty | Leader, ambitious, decisive, intense, results-driven, impatient | Romantic, dreamy, creative, detail-oriented, perfectionist, introspective | Peaceful, diplomatic, loyal, consistent, dislikes conflict and change |
| **Facial cues (per Hallawell)** | Rounded-but-angular mix, mobile expressive features, upturned lines, wide smile | Angular, rectangular face, strong jaw, straight thick brows, deep-set intense eyes | Delicate, elongated features, downward-sloping soft lines, refined details | Round, soft, symmetrical features, gentle curves, serene expression |
| **Design language** | Diagonal lines, movement, texture, asymmetry, mixed shapes | Straight vertical/horizontal lines, sharp angles, geometric precision | Long delicate curves, fine detail, vertical elongation, ornament | Full circles and horizontal curves, smooth continuous forms |
| **Hair shapes / cuts** | Layered cuts with movement, tousled texture, asymmetric or radical/trendy cuts (a sanguine happily accepts a bold change), shags, pixies with texture | Blunt geometric cuts: straight bob, blunt fringe, precise lines, low-maintenance structured styles; strong short cuts | Long, flowing, romantic hair; soft layers; braids, loose buns, boho waves, all types of soft fringes | Rounded, low-angle cuts: soft graduated layers rather than blunt lines; medium lengths; classic, timeless, low-drama styles |
| **Texture / finish** | Visible movement, "undone" finish, waves | Sleek, polished, controlled straight | Loose natural waves, soft romantic finish | Smooth natural finish, gentle bend |
| **Color direction** | Warm blondes, golden tones, bold fashion accents | Deep reds, dark intense monochrome, high contrast (e.g., jet black, vivid copper) | Soft ashy/pearly tones, delicate balayage, rosy pastels | Natural-looking browns, subtle low-contrast color, minimal maintenance color |

*Note:* the dental-visagism literature uses the alias set **strong (choleric), dynamic (sanguine), sensitive/delicate (melancholic), calm (phlegmatic)** and maps them respectively to rectangular, triangular/inclined, oval/soft-vertical, and round tooth/line forms ([WJARR review](https://wjarr.com/sites/default/files/WJARR-2023-0951.pdf)) — a useful confirmation of the shape mapping independent of hair.

**Product implication:** temperament is best captured as a *desired-perception* input rather than an inferred trait — i.e., ask "how do you want to be perceived?" (bold, warm, creative, serene...) and translate to the line-language above. Hallawell himself frames visagism as reconciling "who you are" with "what you want to communicate" ([Ceci Sosa — Visagism in hairdressing](https://cecisosa.com/en/visagism-in-hairdressing/); [Boochi Beauty](https://boochibeauty.com/visagism-define-and-explore-your-beauty/)).

### 1.4 Scientific caveat

The temperament–morphology link is a **tradition-based heuristic, not validated science** (it descends from humoral medicine and physiognomy). What *is* defensible empirically is the softer claim used in social perception research: visual features (angularity vs. curvature, symmetry, grooming) systematically bias trait attributions (dominance, warmth, competence). A product should present temperament language as "style archetypes / perceived vibe," not psychological diagnosis.

## 2. Face-Shape Classification and Canonical Hairstyle Rules

### 2.1 The measurement system

Stylists (and every face-shape calculator) use four measurements ([Beautyblender guide](https://beautyblender.com/blogs/beauty-101/how-to-find-your-face-shape); [Omni Calculator face shape](https://www.omnicalculator.com/health/face-shape); [Face Shapes Chart](https://facedetector.org/face-shapes-chart/); [Totapari facial geometry](https://totapari.com/blogs/news/facial-geometry-and-face-shape-assessment)):

1. **Forehead width (F)** — widest point of forehead, roughly midway between brows and hairline (or temple to temple).
2. **Cheekbone width (C)** — bizygomatic width, across the most prominent points of the cheekbones (outer corner of each eye toward ear).
3. **Jaw width (J)** — across the jaw at its widest, ~1 inch below the ears (gonion to gonion); some systems measure jawline length from chin tip to below ear and double it.
4. **Face length (L)** — hairline (trichion) to chin tip (menton).

Secondary observations: **jaw angle** (rounded vs. angular gonial angle) and **chin shape** (pointed, rounded, flat/square).

### 2.2 Decision rules (the consensus classifier)

Distilled from the calculator/chart sources above ([Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/health/face-shape); [realsmile.online 6-shape classifier](https://realsmile.online/face-shape-calculator); [faceshapedetector.app](https://faceshapedetector.app/face-shapes); [peerlist 7-shapes article](https://peerlist.io/adilbalti/articles/7-face-shapes-explained)):

**Step 1 — length-to-width ratio (L / C):**
- L/C ≈ 1.0–1.15 → "short" family: **round** or **square**
- L/C ≈ 1.3–1.6 → "balanced" family: **oval, heart, diamond** (oval ideal ≈ 1.5)
- L/C ≳ 1.6 (or L much greater than all widths) → **oblong/rectangle**

**Step 2 — widest point:**
- **Cheekbones widest** (F < C > J): oval (soft taper to rounded chin), round (soft jaw, short face), or **diamond** (F and J both notably narrow, pointed chin, angular cheekbones)
- **Forehead widest** (F > C ≥ J): **heart** (tapering to a narrow/pointed chin; often with widow's peak) — sometimes called *inverted triangle* when the hairline is straight
- **Jaw widest** (J > C ≥ F): **triangle / pear**
- **All ≈ equal** (F ≈ C ≈ J): **square** if L ≈ width and angular jaw; **oblong/rectangle** if long

**Step 3 — jaw/chin angularity as tiebreaker:**
- Round vs. square: same proportions; **rounded jaw → round**, **angular jaw → square**
- Oval vs. oblong: oval tapers gently; oblong keeps near-uniform width down the face
- Heart vs. diamond: heart has a **wide forehead**; diamond has a **narrow forehead**

A useful two-question reduction: (a) is the face long or short relative to width? (b) which of forehead/cheeks/jaw dominates? ([Totapari](https://totapari.com/blogs/news/facial-geometry-and-face-shape-assessment)).

### 2.3 The governing aesthetic heuristic

Nearly every styling rule reduces to one principle: **the oval is treated as the reference shape; hair is used to add or remove visual width/length so the framed face reads closer to oval, and to avoid echoing/amplifying the face's dominant feature.** Round face → add height/length, avoid width at cheeks. Long face → add width, avoid height. Square → soften angles. Heart → add jaw-level weight, reduce forehead emphasis. (Whether "oval = ideal" is culturally contingent is worth noting, but it is the operative convention throughout the trade literature: [Luxy Hair guide](https://www.luxyhair.com/blogs/hair-blog/hairstyles-for-different-face-shapes); [Hairlust](https://hairlust.com/blogs/blog/hairstyles-by-face-shape); [Mosaic Hair Studio](https://mosaichairstudio.com/best-haircuts-for-face-shapes/).)

### 2.4 Canonical do's and don'ts per shape

Sources for feminine conventions: [Luxy Hair](https://www.luxyhair.com/blogs/hair-blog/hairstyles-for-different-face-shapes), [Hairlust](https://hairlust.com/blogs/blog/hairstyles-by-face-shape), [Cosmopolitan PH](https://www.cosmo.ph/beauty/hair/best-hairstyle-for-face-shape-src-intl-a5244-20250621-dyn), [BB Salons](https://www.bbsalons.style/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-selecting-the-right-style-for-your-face-shape/). For masculine conventions: [Man of Many barber's guide](https://manofmany.com/style/hairstyles-grooming/choose-hairstyle-face-shape), [FashionBeans](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/how-to-choose-right-haircut-for-face-shape/), [Book of Barbering](https://bookofbarbering.com/face-shapes-guide-men/), [MASC](https://shopmasc.com/blogs/skincare-grooming-101/haircuts-for-men-by-face-shape), [Project Hairway](https://projecthairway.com/blog/choose-perfect-haircut-face-shape), [Clippers Barbershop](https://clippersbarbershop-tx.com/mens-haircuts-by-face-shape/).

**OVAL** (balanced; C widest, L ≈ 1.5×C, soft taper)
- *Goal:* nothing to correct — preserve balance; the face is the versatile baseline.
- *Feminine do:* almost anything — blunt bobs, long layers, all fringe types, slicked-back, pixie. Only caution: heavy full fringe can shorten the face toward round; excessive crown volume can push toward oblong.
- *Masculine do:* nearly universal — quiff, pompadour, crop, buzz, side part, medium flow. *Don't:* heavy forward fringe that shortens the face.

**ROUND** (L ≈ C, full cheeks, rounded jaw)
- *Goal:* elongate; add vertical lines and angles; keep sides close.
- *Feminine do:* long layers below the chin, high crown volume/root lift, deep side part, long side-swept bangs, high ponytails/top knots, asymmetric lob, face-framing pieces below chin level. *Don't:* chin-length blunt bobs (widest point at widest cheek), full straight fringe, center part with flat crown, round halo curls, one-length cuts ending at cheek/chin.
- *Masculine do:* high fade / short sides + height on top: pompadour, quiff, faux hawk, flat top, side part with hard line; light beard sculpting to add jaw angle. *Don't:* bowl cuts, full fringes, uniform round afros/curls left unshaped at the sides.

**SQUARE** (F ≈ C ≈ J, L ≈ width, angular jaw)
- *Goal (feminine convention):* soften the angles; add curve and movement.
- *Feminine do:* soft layers, waves, shags with wispy fringe, side-swept or curtain bangs, lengths below the jaw, texture around the jawline. *Don't:* blunt jaw-length bobs, straight heavy fringe, severe center-parted sleek styles (all echo the squareness).
- *Masculine convention diverges:* the square jaw is prized; cuts either **celebrate** the angularity (buzz, crew cut, side part, undercut — clean geometric lines) or lightly soften it (textured crop, messy quiff). *Don't:* nothing strongly contraindicated; very rounded, heavy full styles can dilute the feature.

**HEART / inverted triangle** (F > C > J, pointed chin)
- *Goal:* narrow the forehead read, add weight at jaw/chin level.
- *Feminine do:* chin-to-shoulder lobs (width at the jaw), curtain bangs or side-swept fringe (cover forehead width without weight), deep side part, waves starting at cheek level, layered ends that kick out at the jaw. *Don't:* very short full-volume-on-top cuts, slicked-back styles exposing the full forehead, chin-length stacked bobs that taper inward (they mimic the chin point), excessive crown volume.
- *Masculine do:* medium-length with fringe (crop with forward fringe, textured fringe), side-swept styles, fuller beard to widen the chin/jaw. *Don't:* high tight fades with big volume on top (exaggerates top-heaviness), skin fades that shrink the lower face further.

**DIAMOND** (C ≫ F ≈ J, narrow forehead and jaw, pointed chin)
- *Goal:* widen forehead and jaw, soften cheekbone prominence.
- *Feminine do:* side-swept or curtain bangs (add forehead width), chin-length bobs with width at the jaw, layers that begin below the cheekbone, tucking hair behind ears only if softness remains around temples. *Don't:* center parts with hair pulled tight (exposes the narrow forehead), volume at cheekbone level, very short slicked styles.
- *Masculine do:* fringes and textured crops (widen forehead), medium length with side volume, longer flow; keep some weight at the temples; beards fill the narrow jaw well. *Don't:* high-and-tight or high fades that narrow the temples further ([Man of Many](https://manofmany.com/style/hairstyles-grooming/choose-hairstyle-face-shape)).

**OBLONG / RECTANGLE** (L ≫ widths; F ≈ C ≈ J; rectangle = angular jaw variant)
- *Goal:* shorten and widen; volume at the sides, never at the crown.
- *Feminine do:* full or curtain bangs (visually shortens the face), waves/curls adding side volume between cheek and jaw, shoulder-length cuts with horizontal weight lines, bobs with fullness. *Don't:* very long one-length straight hair, high crown volume, tall updos/top knots, center part with flat sides.
- *Masculine do:* keep sides fuller (low fade, scissor-cut sides), fringe/French crop to reduce visible forehead, horizontal emphasis; medium flow. *Don't:* pompadours/quiffs with high fades — height plus tight sides makes the face read longer ([FashionBeans](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/how-to-choose-right-haircut-for-face-shape/)).

**TRIANGLE / PEAR** (J > C > F)
- *Goal:* balance the wide jaw by adding volume above it.
- *Feminine do:* volume from crown to cheekbone, layered pixies/shags with textured top, side-swept fringe, lengths either well above or well below the jaw. *Don't:* weight/width at the jawline (chin-length bobs), flat crowns, long straight hair hugging the jaw.
- *Masculine do:* fuller top with moderate sides, textured volume up top, longer fringe-forward styles; avoid emphasizing jaw width with very tight sides + no top volume; a shorter beard rather than a wide full one.

## 3. Beyond Face Shape: Other Facial and Hair Factors

### 3.1 Profile (side view)

Cosmetology's standard (Milady) recognizes **three profiles**: **straight** (treated as ideal), **convex** (receding forehead + receding chin / prominent nose — style hair with fringe moving forward at the forehead and softness at the nape, avoid severe slicked-back looks), and **concave** (prominent forehead and chin, recessed mid-face — keep close soft styling, avoid volume that exaggerates forehead/chin). Milady's design chapters also list nose corrections (prominent nose → bring hair forward with softness; small nose → avoid heavy fringe) and chin corrections (receding chin → volume/movement at jaw level; large chin → hair either above or below the chin line, not at it). Sources: [Milady Principles of Hair Design (slide deck)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/milady-principles-of-hair-design/232045549); [Milady Ch. 14 flashcards](https://quizlet.com/312578177/milady-ch-14-flash-cards/); [Milady Standard Cosmetology](https://archive.org/details/miladystandardco0000unse_f2x4).

### 3.2 Forehead height and hairline shape

- **High forehead** → fringes/bangs (full, curtain, side-swept), forward-textured crops; avoid slicked-back and high pompadours. **Low forehead** → lift hair off the face, volume at the crown, avoid fringe.
- **Hairline taxonomy**: straight, rounded, widow's peak (V-point, ~25–30% prevalence), M-shaped, receding, uneven, high, low, and cowlick-affected ([Scandinavian Biolabs hairline types](https://scandinavianbiolabs.com/blogs/journal/hairline-types); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/hairline-types); [Wimpole — 15 types of hairlines](https://wimpole.com/blog/9-types-of-hairlines-and-how-to-tell-which-one-you-have/)).
- **Widow's peak**: center parts and swept-back styles showcase it; fringes hide it; it fights against blunt straight-across bangs (the point pushes hair apart) ([L'Oréal Paris](https://www.lorealparisusa.com/beauty-magazine/hair-care/all-hair-types/what-is-widows-peak-hairline); [All Things Hair widow's peak guide](https://www.allthingshair.com/en-us/hairstyles-haircuts/hairstyles-for-thin-hair/widows-peak-styling-tips/)).
- **Receding/M-shaped**: textured forward crops, fringe, or embrace with buzz/short cuts; avoid slick-backs and hard parts at the recession point ([HairPalace](https://www.hairpalace.co.uk/blog/hairline-types/)).
- **Cowlicks** (crown or fringe-line) constrain part placement and fringe feasibility; longer hair or added weight masks them; very short cuts must follow the growth pattern.

### 3.3 Neck, ears, glasses, body scale

- **Short neck** → shorter cuts or updos exposing the neck, tapered napes elongate; avoid shoulder-length blunt weight sitting on the shoulders. **Long/thin neck** → hair at or below the neck, soft nape coverage; bobs and lobs flatter. (Milady lists neck length among the physical characteristics an artistic hairstyle must account for — [Milady deck](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/milady-principles-of-hair-design/232045549).)
- **Prominent ears** → keep coverage or weight over/around the ears; avoid tight pulled-back styles, high skin fades, and very short tapers; medium scissor cuts and hair tucked *loosely* work.
- Milady's general camouflage rule: **create volume next to hollow/receding areas, minimize volume next to protruding areas.**
- Overall **body scale and posture** matter: a petite frame is overwhelmed by very big hair; a tall/large frame is unbalanced by tiny close cuts.

### 3.4 Facial thirds, fifths, and the golden ratio

- **Horizontal thirds**: trichion→glabella (brow), glabella→subnasale, subnasale→menton should be roughly equal in the neoclassical canon; hair can rebalance the *upper* third (fringe lowers a tall upper third; lift/volume off the face raises a short one). ([Looksmaxxing wiki — facial thirds](https://looksmaxxingwiki.com/facial-thirds/); [IJFMR review of facial aesthetic proportions](https://www.ijfmr.com/papers/2025/4/53178.pdf).)
- **Vertical fifths**: the face divides into five eye-widths; deviations (wide-set/close-set eyes, narrow temples) inform part placement and side volume.
- **Golden ratio (φ = 1.618)** claims: popular guides map "ideal" L/C ≈ 1.6 and use φ for style proportions (e.g., fringe-to-face ratios; [StyleMyFade golden-ratio haircut](https://stylemyfade.com/blog/the-golden-ratio-and-your-haircut-the-science-of-finding-your-perfect-style); [The Face Report calculator](https://thefacereport.com/tools/golden-ratio)). However, **systematic reviews find no consistent correlation between golden-ratio conformance and rated attractiveness**; surgeons and researchers treat φ as a loose heuristic, not a law ([Dr. Graeme Glass — truth vs myth](https://drgraemeglass.com/blogs/news/truth-and-lies-in-facial-proportions-and-the-golden-ratio); [Medium — what 1.618 actually tells you](https://medium.com/@dicktracey909/the-golden-ratio-in-faces-what-1-618-actually-tells-you-and-what-it-doesnt-09953eb419e8); [Qoves — facial proportionality evidence review](https://www.qoves.com/insights/facial-proportionality)). Product guidance: use thirds/fifths as *adjustment signals*, market nothing as "your golden-ratio score."

### 3.5 Hair type as a hard constraint (Andre Walker 1A–4C + density/porosity)

The **Andre Walker Hair Typing System** (created 1990s by Oprah's stylist Andre Walker) is the industry-standard curl-pattern taxonomy: **Type 1 straight (1A fine/pin-straight, 1B, 1C coarse), Type 2 wavy (2A loose S, 2B, 2C defined waves bordering curls), Type 3 curly (3A large loops → 3C tight corkscrews), Type 4 coily (4A defined coils, 4B Z-pattern, 4C tightest, >75% shrinkage, most fragile)** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Walker_Hair_Typing_System); [hairtypes.org chart](https://hairtypes.org/chart/); [Curl Centric](https://www.curlcentric.com/hair-typing-system/)).

Curl pattern must be crossed with three independent axes — **strand texture** (fine/medium/coarse), **density** (strands per cm²: low/medium/high), and **porosity** (moisture absorption: low/normal/high) — because they jointly determine what a style will actually do ([Theradome hair texture chart](https://theradome.com/blogs/blog/identify-hair-type-with-hair-texture-chart); [hairtypes.org](https://hairtypes.org/)).

Styling constraints (rule-base material):

| Hair reality | Constraint |
|---|---|
| 1A fine straight | Won't hold curls, volume collapses; blunt cuts and sleek styles excel; heavy layers make it stringy |
| 1C / 2A | Most versatile; holds blowouts and waves |
| 2B–2C | Waves must be cut with the pattern (soft layers); blunt one-length can trianglify |
| 3A–3C | Length shrinks 20–50% when dry — cut dry or compensate; layers essential to avoid pyramid shape; blunt fringes risky (curl springs up) |
| 4A–4C | Massive shrinkage (>75% at 4C); styles are shape-sculpted (afro, twist-outs, tapered cuts, protective styles); most fragile — heat/chemical caution highest ([hairtypeschart.com](http://hairtypeschart.com/)) |
| Low density | Avoid long one-length (shows scalp/thinness); blunt shorter cuts add perceived mass |
| High density + coarse | Needs interior weight removal; short precision cuts may expand |
| High porosity | Color grabs fast/fades fast; chemical services riskier |

Key product rule: **the achievable-style set is the intersection of (face-shape-flattering styles) × (styles physically supported by the client's curl pattern, density, texture) × (styles reachable via acceptable chemical/heat intervention)** — and identical curl patterns with different strand textures behave differently ([hairtypes.org](https://hairtypes.org/)).

## 4. Chemical Procedures

All chemical texture services manipulate the **disulfide bonds** of keratin; they differ in whether bonds are permanently broken/reformed or only temporarily loosened/coated.

### 4.1 Relaxers (permanent straightening)
- **Chemistry:** sodium hydroxide (lye), or no-lye calcium hydroxide + guanidine carbonate → guanidine hydroxide; pH ~12–14; permanently breaks disulfide bonds via lanthionization. **Permanent** — new growth requires retouch every 8–12 weeks ([Little Extra Love comparison](https://littleextralove.com/blog/hair-care/relaxers-vs-brazilian-blowout-vs-keratin-treatment); [StyleCraze keratin vs relaxer](https://www.stylecraze.com/articles/keratin-treatment-vs-relaxer/)).
- **Use case:** type 3C–4C clients wanting fully straight or significantly loosened texture ("texlaxing" = deliberate under-processing to loosen, not straighten).
- **Health notes:** highest-damage category; scalp burns if misapplied; incompatible with subsequent perms and risky over bleach; recent epidemiology has linked frequent relaxer use to hormone-related health concerns, pushing many stylists toward keratin systems or natural styling instead.
- **When recommended:** client wants permanent straightness, has healthy virgin or minimally colored hair, accepts retouch maintenance.

### 4.2 Perms (permanent wave/curl)
- **Chemistry:** ammonium thioglycolate (alkaline) or glyceryl monothioglycolate (acid) reduces disulfide bonds; hair is wrapped on rods; neutralizer (H₂O₂) re-forms bonds in the new shape. Lasts **3–6 months**, grows out rather than washing out ([The Look Salon chemical services](https://thelooksalonhouston.com/chemical-services/)).
- **Variants:** body wave (large rods), spiral perm, digital/hot perm (Asian salons — softer, shinier wave), root perms for volume; "reverse perm"/soft straightening uses the same chemistry to relax curl gently.
- **Use case:** adding curl/wave/volume to type 1–2 hair — e.g., giving a round-faced fine-1A client the side volume an oblong correction needs, or enabling "romantic waves" for a melancholic-archetype look.
- **Health notes:** moderate damage; not advised over highlights/bleach; results depend on hair's ability to hold moisture.

### 4.3 Keratin treatments / Brazilian blowout / "progressiva"
- **Chemistry:** keratin/amino-acid solution + aldehyde crosslinker (formalin → **formaldehyde** when heated, or "formaldehyde-free" substitutes like glyoxylic acid), sealed with flat iron at high heat. **Semi-permanent (2–5 months)**, washes out gradually; loosens curl 40–90% and kills frizz rather than making hair permanently straight ([The Hair Dept — Brazilian blowout vs keratin](https://www.thehairdept.com/brazilian-blowout-vs-keratin-whats-the-difference); [Belle Vie Beauty](https://belleviebeautyinc.com/beautyblog/what-is-the-difference-between-a-brazilian-blowout-and-a-keratin-treatment); [Clinikally](https://www.clinikally.com/blogs/news/brazilian-blowout-vs-keratin-treatment-which-is-right-for-you)). In Brazil this is the ubiquitous **escova progressiva** — highly relevant for a Brazilian product.
- **Brazilian Blowout (brand)** coats rather than penetrates, no downtime, ~10–12 weeks; classic keratin penetrates deeper, lasts longer, may have wash/tie restrictions for 72h ([Live By The Sword Salon](https://www.livebytheswordsalon.com/brazilian-blowout-vs-keratin-treatment); [Hair Pinns](https://hairpinns.com/blog/keratin-vs-brazilian-blowout-vs-straight-up/)).
- **Health notes:** formaldehyde is a recognized human carcinogen; the FDA has moved toward banning formaldehyde-releasing smoothers — prefer glyoxylic-acid ("acid organic") systems; protein-overload brittleness is possible on fine or low-porosity hair ([Little Extra Love](https://littleextralove.com/blog/hair-care/relaxers-vs-brazilian-blowout-vs-keratin-treatment)).
- **When recommended:** frizz reduction and manageability for 2C–4A hair, clients wanting reversible smoothing, color-treated hair (gentler than relaxer).

### 4.4 Texturizers and other services
- **Texturizer:** a mild, short-application relaxer that loosens (not straightens) coils — turns 4B/4C into a softer defined curl; permanent, same chemistry class as relaxers.
- **Carving/texturizing cuts and chemical texturizing** are the stylist's tools for reconciling a target silhouette with natural pattern.
- **Bond-builders (Olaplex-type)** are recommended alongside/after any of the above to limit disulfide damage.

**Rule-base relevance:** chemical services are the *bridge edges* in the recommendation graph — a style outside the client's natural-texture reach becomes reachable at a cost (damage risk, maintenance cadence, price), which should be surfaced as a trade-off, and blocked entirely when hair condition is poor (bleached + relaxer, etc.).

## 5. Encoding This as a Recommendation Rule Base

### 5.1 Required inputs

**A. Facial landmark measurements** (from a front photo via a landmark model — e.g., MediaPipe FaceMesh 468 points — plus optional profile photo):

| Measurement | Landmarks | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Face length **L** | trichion → menton | shape, thirds |
| Forehead width **F** | left/right temporal crest | shape |
| Bizygomatic width **C** | left/right zygion | shape (denominator) |
| Bigonial width **J** | left/right gonion | shape |
| Jaw angle **θ** | curvature/angle at gonion | round-vs-square, oblong-vs-rectangle |
| Chin shape | contour curvature at menton | heart/diamond vs oval |
| Upper/mid/lower thirds | trichion, glabella, subnasale, menton | forehead height correction |
| Eye spacing (fifths) | canthi positions | part placement |
| Hairline class | contour of trichion line | fringe/part feasibility |
| Profile class (optional) | side photo: forehead/nose/chin sagittal | volume placement front/back |
| Symmetry index | left/right landmark deltas | asymmetric-cut suitability |
| Ear prominence (optional) | ear contour vs head silhouette | short-cut caution flag |
| Neck length (optional) | menton → shoulder line | length-band adjustments |

**B. Hair attributes:** curl pattern (1A–4C), strand texture (fine/med/coarse), density (low/med/high), porosity, current length, current chemical history/condition, growth quirks (cowlicks, whorls), gray %.

**C. Preference/context:** desired perceived personality (multi-select with weights: bold, creative, approachable/warm, professional, elegant, romantic, edgy, serene...), maintenance tolerance (minutes/day, salon visits/quarter), openness to chemical services and heat, length constraints (job/religious/personal), gender presentation of styling conventions to apply (feminine/masculine/neutral — decoupled from identity).

### 5.2 Face-shape classifier (concrete thresholds)

Normalize widths by C. Let `r = L/C`, `f = F/C`, `j = J/C`.

```
if r < 1.15:
    shape = SQUARE if jaw_angularity > τ_ang else ROUND
elif r > 1.6 and max(f, j) > 0.9:          # near-uniform widths, long
    shape = RECTANGLE if jaw_angularity > τ_ang else OBLONG
else:  # 1.15 ≤ r ≤ 1.6
    if f > 1.0 and j < 0.85 and chin == pointed:  shape = HEART
    elif f < 0.85 and j < 0.85:                    shape = DIAMOND
    elif j > 1.0 and j > f + 0.1:                  shape = TRIANGLE
    else:                                          shape = OVAL
```

Better than hard argmax: output a **soft membership vector** (e.g., distances to shape prototypes in (r, f, j, angularity, chin) space, softmaxed) — real faces are blends, and downstream scoring should weight rules by membership. (Thresholds calibrated against the calculator conventions: oval L/C ≈ 1.5, round/square L ≈ C, oblong L ≥ 1.5–1.6×C — [Omni Calculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/health/face-shape), [realsmile.online](https://realsmile.online/face-shape-calculator), [facedetector.org](https://facedetector.org/face-shapes-chart/).)

### 5.3 Style attribute schema

Encode every hairstyle in the catalog as a feature vector, not a name:

```yaml
style:
  id: textured_lob_curtain_bangs
  length_band: shoulder            # buzz|short|ear|chin|shoulder|long
  silhouette: {width_at_temples: +1, width_at_cheeks: 0, width_at_jaw: +2, crown_height: 0}
  line_language: {straightness: -1, curvature: +2, diagonality: +1, angularity: -2}  # -2..+2
  fringe: curtain                  # none|full_blunt|curtain|side_swept|micro|textured_forward
  part: flexible                   # center|side|flexible|none
  forehead_coverage: partial
  symmetry: symmetric
  texture_required: [2A, 2B, 2C, 3A]      # natural patterns that support it
  texture_reachable_via: {blowout: [1B,1C], perm: [1A], keratin: [3B,3C]}
  density_min: medium
  maintenance: {daily_min: 10, salon_weeks: 8}
  formality: 0.6                   # 0 casual .. 1 formal
  archetype_scores: {bold: 0.3, creative: 0.5, warm: 0.8, professional: 0.6, elegant: 0.6, romantic: 0.7, edgy: 0.2, serene: 0.5}
```

The `silhouette` deltas are the key innovation: they state *where the style adds or removes visual mass*, which lets face-shape scoring be computed rather than hand-enumerated per (shape × style) pair.

### 5.4 Scoring model sketch

**Total score for style s given client c:**

```
Score(s, c) = w_h · Harmony(s, c)          # face-shape geometric fit
            + w_p · Personality(s, c)      # visagism archetype match
            + w_f · Feasibility(s, c)      # hair-type reachability
            + w_x · Context(s, c)          # maintenance, lifestyle, corrections
            − Penalties(s, c)              # hard-rule violations
```

Suggested default weights: w_h = 0.35, w_p = 0.30, w_f = 0.25, w_x = 0.10 — user-tunable ("prioritize how it flatters" vs "prioritize the vibe").

**Harmony(s, c)** — computed from correction targets. Each face shape defines a target adjustment vector `T(shape)` over the same silhouette axes:

| Shape | temples | cheeks | jaw | crown height | angularity target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oval | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | free |
| Round | 0 | −2 | 0 | +2 | +1 (add angles) |
| Square | 0 | 0 | −1 | +1 | −2 (soften)* |
| Heart | −1 | 0 | +2 | −1 | −1 |
| Diamond | +2 | −2 | +1 | 0 | −1 |
| Oblong | +1 | +2 | +1 | −2 | 0 |
| Triangle | +2 | +1 | −2 | +2 | 0 |

\* masculine convention flips square's angularity target to 0/+1 (celebrate the jaw) — keep a per-convention table.

Then `Harmony = 1 − Σ_axis |T_axis − s.silhouette_axis| / max_dist`, weighted by soft shape-membership: `Σ_shape m(shape) · Harmony_shape(s)`. Add modifier terms: forehead-height rule (tall upper third → bonus for `forehead_coverage ∈ {partial, full}`), hairline compatibility (widow's peak → penalty for `fringe = full_blunt`, bonus for center part), ear/neck flags.

**Personality(s, c)** — cosine similarity between the user's desired-perception weight vector and the style's `archetype_scores`. The archetype scores themselves are *derived* from line language via the visagism mapping, keeping the knowledge base compact:

```
bold        = 0.5·straightness + 0.7·angularity + 0.3·crown_height
creative    = 0.7·diagonality + 0.6·asymmetry + 0.4·texture_visible
warm        = 0.8·curvature + 0.4·softness_of_fringe − 0.3·angularity
professional= 0.6·control/sleekness + 0.4·symmetry + formality
elegant     = 0.5·curvature + 0.5·length + sleekness − visible_mess
romantic    = 0.8·curvature + 0.5·length + soft_fringe   (melancholic)
serene      = 0.7·roundness + low_contrast_color          (phlegmatic)
edgy/dynamic= 0.6·diagonality + 0.5·contrast + undercut    (sanguine/choleric mix)
```

This is exactly Hallawell's temperament grammar operationalized: choleric↔bold/professional (straight+angular), sanguine↔dynamic/creative (diagonal+textured), melancholic↔romantic/elegant (long curves+detail), phlegmatic↔serene/approachable (round+smooth). Color recommendations plug into the same vector (choleric: deep/high-contrast; sanguine: warm golden; melancholic: soft ash/pastel; phlegmatic: natural low-contrast).

**Feasibility(s, c)** — 1.0 if the client's (pattern, texture, density) ∈ `texture_required`; else the best score over `texture_reachable_via` discounted by a chemical-cost factor: `0.8` for blowout/styling, `0.6` for semi-permanent (keratin/progressiva), `0.4` for permanent (perm/relaxer), `0` if client declined chemicals or hair-condition flags (bleached + relaxer request) trigger a hard block. Multiply by a shrinkage-awareness check for 3C–4C (style length bands interpreted on stretched vs dry length).

**Penalties / hard rules (examples):**
- Chin-length blunt bob × round face membership > 0.6 → −large
- High fade + pompadour × oblong membership > 0.6 → −large ([FashionBeans](https://www.fashionbeans.com/article/how-to-choose-right-haircut-for-face-shape/))
- Full blunt fringe × strong widow's peak or fringe-line cowlick → −large
- Relaxer request × severely compromised hair → hard block with explanation
- Slicked-back × convex profile + high forehead → −medium

**Output:** ranked list with *explanations generated from the fired rules* ("adds width at the jaw to balance your wider forehead; the curved layers read warm and approachable — matching your 'friendly + professional' goal; works with your 2B waves with a 10-min routine"). Explainability is the product moat here — every rule above has a human-readable rationale straight from the trade literature.

**Validation path:** the geometric rules are conventions, not physics — plan to tune weights with stylist-labeled examples and user A/B feedback; keep the temperament layer framed as "style archetypes," and treat golden-ratio content as optional flavor, not a score (per the evidence reviews: [Dr. Glass](https://drgraemeglass.com/blogs/news/truth-and-lies-in-facial-proportions-and-the-golden-ratio), [Qoves](https://www.qoves.com/insights/facial-proportionality)).
